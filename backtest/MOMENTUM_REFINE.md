# Refining the momentum strategy: does taking profits help? (2017-01-31 to 2026-11-12)

Generated by `python -m backtest.momentum_refine` on 2026-11-13. See the module docstring for the method.

Base: the momentum paper account's original rules. At the last close of each month it picks the 10 stocks among the 500 largest with the best 6-1 month return and holds them at 10% each from the next open (10 bps a side, idle cash earns T-bills). Since 2026-11-13 the paper account trades the 'keep a holding while it ranks in the top 30' variant. Every variant changes one thing. Each runs ten times, with the monthly trade 0, 2, ..., 18 sessions after month end, and the tables show the average of the ten: a 10-stock portfolio's result depends a lot on the trading day. 'Timings better' counts the trading days at which the variant beat the base over the whole period.

## Answer

**Taking profits at a set level did not raise returns.** Of 44 take-profit, trailing-stop, 'stretched' and stop-loss settings, 4 beat the original rules on average, by at most +0.3% a year ('At +150%, cash until the month end', better at 6 of 10 trading days), which is within the noise. None was better in both 2017-21 and 2022-26 and at 8 or more of the 10 trading days. Selling everything at +10% to +50% changed the yearly return by -5.6% to -0.4%, and trailing stops by -11.7% to -1.1%. The levels that looked best in 2017-21 did not hold up in 2022-26 (table below). The reason: most of the profit comes from a few holdings that keep running long after they are up 50% or 100%, and a profit target sells exactly those.

**What helped was the opposite: holding winners longer.** Selling a holding only when it drops out of the top 30 (instead of the top 10) returned 31.1% a year on average against 28.5%, better at 8 of 10 trading days and in both 2017-21 (+0.8%) and 2022-26 (+4.2%), with a smaller drawdown and 39% less trading. Every cut-off from the top 20 to the top 50 helped (+0.9% to +2.5% a year). But the gain is lumpy: it came mostly from 2020 (+23%) and 2024 (+14%), and the buffer trailed the original rules in 5 of the 10 calendar years (table below). It won those years mostly by holding on to big winners the original rules sold early: Tesla, DocuSign and Nvidia in 2020, AppLovin and Vertiv in 2024. Leaving winners at their grown weight instead of trimming them back to 10% at each rebalance raised the average to 32.6% (+4.0%; better at 10 of 10 trading days and in both periods), but then one stock reached 47% of the account (original rules: 19%), so the return rests on a few stocks.

**Expect less than the headline.** The paper account trades at the month end, which returned 33.6% a year with the original rules, the best of the ten; the average over the ten trading days was 28.5% (worst 22.2%). SPY returned 14.8% (18.2% in 2017-21, 11.6% in 2022-26). Survivorship bias and the choice of these rules from about 100 settings flatter every strategy number here.

## Why selling winners early costs money

Positions of the original rules (month-end timing) that closed at least this far above their purchase price, and what they did from the next open until the strategy actually sold them:

| Gain since purchase | Positions that got there | Then rose further | Median further move | Average further move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +20% | 154 of 597 | 55% | +2% | +10% |
| +50% | 45 of 597 | 49% | -2% | +20% |
| +100% | 14 of 597 | 64% | +17% | +31% |

After the target is hit roughly half rise further and half fall back, but the average further move is large because a few keep running a long way. A profit target sells those too.

## Caveats

- Survivorship bias: the data holds only stocks listed today, which flatters momentum (by about 1% a year for the largest 500 at equal weight, more for a concentrated portfolio).
- The base was itself picked from about 100 momentum settings in the strategy search (backtest/STRATEGIES.md), so its level is optimistic; comparisons between variants of it are fairer than its absolute return.
- Ten years, mostly a bull market led by large US tech. Profit taking and stops would look better in a market that rises and then gives it all back (2000-02), which is not in the sample.
- Signals use closing prices and trade at the next open; a stop resting at the exchange would fill during the day, sometimes better, sometimes worse after a gap.
