## Verdict

The rule worked before the years it was chosen on: 17.6% a year in 2000-2015 against 3.9% for the S&P 500, and the index's bounce after closes at the low was as strong then as now. It came through the dot-com bust (-49%) where holding a 3x fund was wiped out and the 200-day rule in a 3x fund lost 90%. But it fell 73% at its worst and took years to recover, much deeper than the London test since 2016 showed. Buying half the usual amount while the index is below its 200-day average keeps most of the return (17.3% a year since 2000, 26.3% on the London funds since 2017) and cuts the worst fall to 50% (London: 23%). It is a choice of risk, not a better rule: it was chosen after seeing the long history, so its numbers are not an independent test.
